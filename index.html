Variable Reference Guide

Comprehensive physics breakdown of each variable governing aerodynamic forces.

kg / m³

Air Density (ρ)

Mass of air per unit volume. Density decreases with higher altitude or temperature, directly scaling total aerodynamic lift and drag forces.

m / s (km/h)

True Airspeed (v)

The relative velocity between the aircraft wing and oncoming fluid stream. Forces increase with the square of velocity ($v^2$).

m²

Wing Reference Area (S)

Total projected planform area of the wing surface. A larger surface area captures greater fluid momentum.

Dimensionless

Lift Coefficient (CL)

Dimensionless parameter encapsulating geometry, camber, and angle of attack. Determines how effectively dynamic pressure turns into lift.

Dimensionless

Drag Coefficient (CD)

Quantifies total fluid resistance, combining parasitic skin friction ($C_{D0}$) and lift-induced vortex drag ($C_{Di}$).

Degrees (°)

Angle of Attack (α)

The geometric pitch angle between the chord line and relative wind direction. Increasing $\alpha$ boosts lift up to critical stall.

Pascals (Pa)

Dynamic Pressure (q)

Kinetic energy per unit volume of the moving fluid flow field, calculated precisely as $q = \frac{1}{2} \rho v^2$.

Ratio

Lift-to-Drag Ratio (L/D)

The primary index of aerodynamic efficiency. Represents distance flown horizontally per unit altitude loss in gliding flight.

4 Core Aerodynamic Principles

The four cornerstone fluid dynamics theories that explain mechanical flight.

1. Bernoulli's Principle

Fluid velocity increases as air travels over the curved upper crest of an airfoil. By conservation of energy, this local flow acceleration lowers static surface pressure above the wing, producing upward suction.

2. Newton's Third Law

For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction. An angled airfoil physically forces the oncoming air stream downwards (downwash). The air exerts an equal force upward against the wing structure.

3. Circulation & Kutta-Joukowski

Lift is proportional to fluid circulation ($\Gamma$) around a closed airfoil path. Smooth flow off the sharp trailing edge enforces the Kutta condition, creating bound vortex circulation that generates lift force.

4. Boundary Layer & Stall Dynamics

Viscous friction creates a boundary layer against the wing skin. Beyond a critical angle of attack ($\alpha_{\text{crit}} \approx 15^\circ$), adverse pressure gradients cause flow separation, turbulence, and sudden loss of lift.
